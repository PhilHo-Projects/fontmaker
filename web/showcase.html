What a paragraph knows that a logo never will

By Ada Whitmore · 27 July 2026 · 9 min

A typeface shows its hand slowly. You can admire a letterform in isolation for an afternoon and still have no idea whether it can hold a page together at eleven pixels on a tired screen.

The test is boring on purpose. Set four hundred words, stand back far enough that the words stop being words, and look at the grey. It should be even. No word should sit darker than its neighbours, no line should sag, and the spaces between words should be quieter than the spaces between lines. If the page flickers, something is wrong — usually the spacing, and only rarely the drawing.

Rhythm before beauty

Stems make the rhythm. In minimum the eye reads the beat of the verticals before it reads the letters, and if one gap is wider than the rest the whole word stumbles. This is why spacing is drawn, not typed: a round letter needs less room than a flat one because its extreme is a single tangent point rather than a whole edge, and a diagonal needs less again because its serifs should interlock with what comes next.

Type is a beautiful group of letters, not a group of beautiful letters.

— Matthew Carter

Which is a way of saying that the letters you fuss over — the ampersand, the swash, the clever g — are the ones that matter least. Nobody reads an ampersand. They read the and of and and, several thousand times, and those are made of the letters nobody sketches on a Sunday.

Three things to check first

  1. The x-height against the cap height. Too small and the page turns grey and distant; too large and the ascenders lose the word shapes that make skimming possible.
  2. The apertures of c, e and s. When they close up, the letters start to look like o at small sizes and reading slows.
  3. The numerals, which everyone forgets until the first table: 0123456789 in a price, a date, a phone number.

None of this is visible in a specimen poster, which is the point. A display face is judged in a second; a text face is judged over an hour, and the reader never consciously notices either the success or the failure — only whether they got to the end.

So: set the paragraph. Squint. If the grey is even and nothing jumps, the face works, and everything else is preference. Read the next essay →
